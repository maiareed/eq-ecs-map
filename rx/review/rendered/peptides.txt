What is a peptide, really?

A peptide is a short chain of amino acids, the same building blocks that make up proteins. The difference is length. Here's how the molecules you'll see at Equilibria compare, bead for bead.

Peptides are suddenly everywhere: in skincare, on podcasts, in the group chat. The same word gets used for a face cream and an injection, which makes it hard to know what you're actually looking at.

Size is the simplest way in. Each bead below is one amino acid. Count the beads and you know what kind of molecule you're dealing with. You'll also see why the word peptide on its own tells you so little.

Bead for bead

One bead is one amino acid, and each full line holds 40. That's the FDA's cutoff: a chain longer than 40 counts as a protein.

Three things the beads tell you
Peptide describes size, not effect

The word tells you a molecule is a short chain. It doesn't tell you what the chain does, how well it's been studied, or whether it suits you. That's what each explainer is for.[9]

Established
Length is only the first detail

Some short chains are closed into rings, like PT-141 and oxytocin. Some are paired with something else, like the copper in GHK-Cu.[3][4][2]

Established
Not every Rx is a peptide

NAD+ and naltrexone are small molecules, not chains. If someone calls everything in this category a peptide, you now know better.[11][12]

Established
How sure is this?

Every claim on this page carries one of four line styles. The heavier the line, the stronger the evidence.

Established. Large human trials, an FDA drug label, or settled biology.
Early human studies. Small or early studies in people. Worth knowing, still being confirmed.
Lab and animal research. Studies in cells or animals. Clues about how something might work in people.
Leading theory. The best current explanation. Researchers are still testing it.

KPV

3 beads

A three-bead fragment from the end of a longer hormone called alpha-MSH.[1]

Established

GHK

3 beads

The peptide in GHK-Cu: three beads paired with copper, first isolated from human plasma in 1973. In Radiance Cream Rx and Copper Glow Cream Rx.[2]

Established

PT-141

7 beads

Also called bremelanotide. Seven beads, six of them closed into a ring, including two building blocks you won't find in everyday proteins. Part of Intimacy Melt Rx, a compounded preparation that isn't an FDA-approved product.[3]

Established

Oxytocin

9 beads

Nine beads, with a small ring formed by two linked building blocks. Part of Intimacy Melt Rx.[4]

Established

BPC-157

15 beads

A lab-made chain of fifteen, described as derived from a protein in gastric juice. In Gut Peptides Rx and Wolverine Recovery Rx.[5]

Established

MOTS-c

16 beads

Sixteen beads, coded in mitochondrial DNA rather than nuclear DNA.[6]

Established

Tirzepatide

39 beads

Thirty-nine beads, just under the FDA's 40-bead line, so it's regulated as a peptide rather than a protein. Used in our compounded GLP/GIP Rx options, which aren't FDA-approved products.[7]

Established

Insulin

51 beads

Two chains of 21 and 30 beads, counted together. That puts insulin over the line, so the FDA regulates it as a protein. Shown for scale.[8][9]

Established

Serum albumin

585 beads

A blood protein, 585 beads long once it's fully made. It's here so you can see what a full-size protein looks like next to a peptide. Shown for scale.[10]

Established

NAD+

Not a chain

NAD+ isn't made of amino acids at all. It's a dinucleotide, two nucleotides joined together. The active ingredient in Energy Spray Rx.[11]

Established

Naltrexone

Not a chain

A classic small-molecule medicine, and the active ingredient in LDN Rx.[12]
